Exam strategy

What actually wins (or loses) CLB 5

At CLB 5, nobody expects advanced French. What is expected is a task that is complete, structured, and understandable. Here is where the points are won, task by task. The French formulas below are the exact phrases to memorize and reuse.

The 3 rules that outrank everything else: 1) address every part of the prompt — an incomplete task caps your score; 2) respect the word count and the time limit; 3) prefer simple, correct sentences over complex, faulty ones.

✍️ Writing — winning structures

Formal letter (TEF Section B)

  • Opening: Monsieur le Maire, / Madame, Monsieur,
  • State your purpose in the first sentence: Je vous écris au sujet de...
  • Clear opinion: Je ne suis pas d'accord avec ce projet parce que...
  • 2–3 arguments, each introduced by D'abord, De plus, Enfin
  • A solution: Je propose de...
  • Closing formula: Je vous prie d'agréer, Monsieur le Maire, mes salutations distinguées.

Past-tense narration (TEF Section A, TCF Task 2)

  • Imparfait for the setting: Il faisait froid, la rue était calme...
  • Passé composé for the events: Soudain, un arbre est tombé...
  • Link with d'abord, ensuite, puis, finalement
  • Always end with a concluding sentence or a feeling.

Comparison + opinion (TCF Task 3)

  • Selon le premier document, ... / En revanche, le deuxième document affirme que...
  • Then: À mon avis, ... + one argument + one personal example.
  • Never copy the documents: rephrase in your own words.

🎤 Speaking — winning reflexes

Asking questions (TEF Section A, TCF Task 2)

  • Prepare a mental grid: price? schedule? location? registration? equipment? level? instructor?
  • Vary the question forms: Est-ce que... ?, Quel est... ?, Combien coûte... ?, Pourriez-vous me dire... ?
  • React between questions: Ah très bien !, D'accord, parfait.
  • Open and close politely: Bonjour, je vous appelle au sujet de... / Merci beaucoup, au revoir !

Convincing / giving an opinion (TEF Section B, TCF Task 3)

  • State your position: Je pense que c'est une excellente idée parce que...
  • Arguments with examples: Par exemple, la semaine dernière...
  • To convince: Tu devrais venir, Je t'assure que..., C'est l'occasion de...
  • Handling objections: C'est vrai, mais..., Je comprends, cependant...

Fluency: the pace rule

  • A short hesitation is normal; a 5-second silence is expensive.
  • Buy time naturally: Alors..., Eh bien..., C'est une bonne question.
  • Don't self-correct more than once per sentence: keep going.

❌ The mistakes that cost you CLB 5

  • Partially off-topic: answering only one half of a two-part prompt (describe AND invite, summarize AND give your opinion).
  • Wrong register: using "tu" with the mayor, or "vous" with your best friend. Check who the recipient is before writing the first sentence.
  • Ignoring the word count: 40 words when 60 minimum are required = incomplete task.
  • Wrong auxiliary in the passé composé: je suis allé, but j'ai mangé. Learn the list of verbs that take "être".
  • One-word answers in speaking: every question deserves 2–3 sentences, even a simple one.
  • Memorized speeches: examiners detect them immediately and penalize them.

📆 Express preparation plan

Phase 1 — Foundations

  • Secure the present tense, passé composé/imparfait, and futur proche.
  • Memorize the formal letter and friendly message formulas.
  • Learn 20–30 words per high-frequency topic: housing, work, services, leisure, transport.

Phase 2 — Exam format

  • One timed practice task per day, alternating writing and speaking.
  • After each task: self-assessment, then fix only your 2–3 main errors.

Phase 3 — Real conditions

  • Do a full skill exam (all tasks) without a break.
  • Record your speaking answers and listen back 24 hours later.
  • Goal: pass every task at "CLB 5 reached" twice in a row before exam day.
