Choosing your exam

TEF Canada or TCF Canada?

Both exams are accepted by IRCC. Pick the one whose format suits you best — then train exclusively on that format.

TEF CanadaTCF Canada
Writing 2 sections: continue a news story (~100 words) + argumentative letter (~200 words), 60 min 3 progressive tasks: short message (60–120 words), personal experience (120–150), comparison + opinion (120–180), 60 min
Speaking 2 face-to-face sections: asking for information (5 min) + convincing someone (10 min) 3 tasks: guided interview (2 min), interaction (5 min 30 incl. preparation), point of view (4 min 30, no preparation)
Production scoring Score 0–699 per skill Mark 0–20 per skill
CLB 5 threshold (writing / speaking) 330 / 387 (post-Dec 2023 scale) 6 / 6 out of 20
Best suited for Candidates comfortable with past-tense narration and formal letters Candidates who prefer short, progressive tasks

Sources: official IRCC equivalency tables (canada.ca). See the IRCC Tables page for all skills and all levels.

How the assessment works

An examiner's evaluation — deliberately strict

🔍

1. The official criteria

Every production is judged on the dimensions that are actually scored: task achievement, coherence, vocabulary, grammar, and register (writing) or fluency and pronunciation (speaking).

🧮

2. The official conversion

Your estimated score is converted to an NCLC level using the current IRCC tables — including the TEF scale that applies to tests taken after December 10, 2023.

⚖️

3. A verdict with no sugar-coating

You know immediately whether your answer reaches CLB 5 — and if not, exactly which 2 or 3 fixes would push it over the line.

Golden rule: an off-topic or incomplete answer caps your score below CLB 5, even with excellent French. Task achievement comes before everything else.
